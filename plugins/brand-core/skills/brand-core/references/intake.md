# Intake: what to ask for before starting

Ask once, in one compact message, for everything that is missing. Do not start measuring until you know which sources are the brand's **own** material (published pieces, guideline sheet, docs) and which are **references** (boards, folders of inspiration). The two get different jobs on the page, and mixing them is the most expensive mistake to undo.

## Required

| Material | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **At least one reference set**: public Pinterest board URLs or a folder of images. Ideally one board per job: social layouts, photography per "world" (city, nature, product, people), graphics/identity, long-form, video. | It is the "referenced" column of every comparison, and the format map is built from it. One board per job lets each board answer one question; a single mixed board has to be split by eye first. |
| **Either already-published pieces** (PDF or per-slide PNG exports of carousels, video files, screenshots of the live profile) **or an existing guideline sheet** (PNG/PDF). | Without one of them, there is nothing the brand has actually committed to. Published pieces show what is really used; a guideline sheet shows what was declared. |
| **One paragraph of positioning**: what the brand offers *now*, for whom (age, place, language), and where the audience meets it (which channels). | Decides which formats and content series matter, and which old material describes an offer that no longer exists. |

## Strongly recommended

| Material | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Published pieces AND the guideline sheet**, both. | The gap between what was declared and what was published is where most findings come from: an accent used in every post but missing from the sheet, a declared serif that no post uses, a label that does not match its swatch. |
| **Brand docs**: voice rules, banned words, taglines, content series, audience tensions or pains. | Maps formats to series, gives the copy samples for the type voices, and supplies the "written" side of the conflicts. |
| **Logo / mascot files** (SVG preferred). | The logo's real role (lockup only? a character?) is usually an open decision. |
| **Channel list with intended formats**: feed, reels, stories, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube, ebook/guide, landing page, app store, print. | The format map only covers channels someone will actually make. |

## Context questions (ask them in the same message)

1. Did the product or business change recently? Material made before the change may describe the old offer (old CTA, old product screens, old prices). It still counts as evidence of the visual system, but gets flagged.
2. When sources conflict, which one wins by default: the guideline sheet, the published pieces, or neither?
3. Who decides, and who else will review the page? (Decides whether the page needs a shared decision log.)
4. Which fonts are licensed? (A face you cannot license is not a recommendation; show it as a stand-in.)
5. Which design tool produces the pieces (Affinity, Canva, Figma, Photoshop)? (Decides how specs and tokens are handed over.)
6. Which language is the brand's copy in? (Every sample line on the page is written in it.)

## Minimum viable run

One reference set + the positioning paragraph. Run the full method, but the page must say clearly, in the hero and next to every palette and type conclusion, that they rest on references only: nothing has been checked against what the brand declared or published. Skip the "already made" and "guidelines" sections (delete them and their nav links from the template).

## Message template

Adapt the wording to the person and the language they write in. Keep it to one message.

> To build the brand core I need a few things. Required:
> 1. **References**: the public Pinterest board URLs (or a folder of images). Best if each board has one job: post layouts, photos per world, graphics, long-form, video.
> 2. **What the brand already made and/or declared**: exports of published carousels (per-slide PNGs or PDFs), video files or profile screenshots, and/or the brand guideline sheet.
> 3. **Positioning, one paragraph**: what you offer now, for whom (age, place, language), and where they meet you.
>
> If you have them, these make the result much sharper: both the published pieces *and* the guideline sheet, brand docs (voice, taglines, content series), the logo as SVG, and the channels you plan to post on.
>
> And quick context: did the product change recently? When sources disagree, which wins? Who decides, and who else will review this? Which fonts are licensed? Which tool makes the pieces? What language is the copy in?

## After the answers

Write down, before collecting anything:

- the list of sources, each marked **own** or **reference**, with a short slug (`posts-board`, `city`, `published`, `guidelines`);
- the channels in scope;
- the content series (from the docs), so sub-formats can be mapped to them later;
- what changed recently, so old-offer material can be flagged instead of discarded.
